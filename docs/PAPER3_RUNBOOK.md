## 1. Short version

1. **Once:** one campaign commit that every host checks out, pushed; the harness smoke test (design 7.2) recorded; the operator machine
   set up (section 3). B200 is admitted (design amendment A2, section 3 item 3).
2. **Per launch** (11 launches: A100, H100, GH200, B200 and C-GCP twice each, C-VM once; 9 if B200 never finds capacity):
   `p3_price_snapshot.sh` -> `p3_launch.sh` (dry run, then `--yes`) -> `p3_driver.sh RECORD` in the background -> short looks with
   `p3_status.sh` -> the driver copies every round off the host with checksums, tears the host down and confirms it.
3. **Order:** H100 launch 1 and C-GCP launch 1 first (the pathfinders); the other hosts start once the H100's round 1 has passed its
   gates and its copy is verified; launch 2 of a host after its launch 1; C-GCP launch 2 after the metal host is deleted (quota).
   About 2.7 days of wall time (B200 included: its two launches end inside the A100's) and the design's ~$1,364 core estimate plus about $22 to $30 of round pauses the design did not count.
4. **C-GCP** stops once, after bring-up, for a person to review what is scheduled on the host (section 6).
5. **Nobody looks at a timing, energy, power or cost aggregate until every round is done or stopped** (design 7.3). The tools print only
   states, counts and gate outcomes, and the QA view cannot open anything else (section 8).

* Hosts of different classes run in parallel. Lambda's limits on concurrent instances are not documented in the repository (unverified):

* **GCP quota:** `C3_CPUS` is 300 per region in us-east4 and us-central1, so one 192-vCPU metal host fits per region and two do not. The

  launch 1 is deleted (or in the other region, which has its own quota and is in the price table).
* **Pathfinders first:** the H100 is the reference host and the cheapest place to find a harness or runbook fault; C-GCP is the most

* B200 (admitted by amendment A2) starts at the pathfinder gate with the A100 and the GH200: about 24 h per launch, $7.99/h, two launches, in the timeline

* **The pathfinder gate, exactly:** H100 launch 1 has bring-up verified, the harness smoke test recorded, and round 1 accepted by the driver
  (its copy verified), and the QA view of `round-1` shows gate verdict `pass` with no G6 or G12 failure. Then A100, GH200 and C-VM launch 1 start

  rounds (gates only). It uses the same CAMPAIGN commit (C-GCP: the same CONFIG commit) and the same instance type; a different region or
  zone is fine and is recorded (between-instance variation is the point of two launches, design 3.2).

## 4. One launch, step by step

**Guardrails, each from a failure we already had** (they are why the tools are built the way they are):

* *Never trust one long SSH call.* The 2026-11-16 B200 battery lost four of its five remote stages to `ssh` exit 255 when the driving Mac
  slept. Every stage here is detached on the host and polled with short, time-bounded calls; a poll that fails is retried.
* *Detach with one simple command.* `a && b && nohup c > /dev/null 2>&1 & disown` backgrounds the whole AND-list, whose subshell still holds the
  SSH channel: the GCP P6-CPU start call lasted as long as the probe (17 minutes). `p3_detach.sh` backgrounds a single command with every
  descriptor redirected, and finishes a stage exactly when its exit-code file exists.
* *A watchdog counts wall-clock time, not sleep time.* `sleep 5400` does not tick while the Mac sleeps; the B200 run outlived its 5400 s
  backstop by hours. The watchdogs compare the clock with a recorded epoch deadline every 30 s, so they fire at the first wake past it. They
  are still Mac processes: they cannot act while the Mac is off (section 3, item 4).
* *`caffeinate`* holds the Mac awake while the driver and the watchdog live (`caffeinate -i -s -w PID`); a closed lid or battery power can still
  sleep it, which the polling survives.
* *A failed listing is not "gone".* Termination is confirmed only by a successful listing that no longer shows the instance.
* *Shared accounts.* The Lambda account and the GCP project hold instances that are not ours. Every command acts on one recorded id or exact
  name, `lambda.sh` refuses names not starting with `p3-`, and every GCP listing filters on `labels.project=paper3-energy`.
* *A lease on every instance*: owner and expiry in the name, checked by `run_campaign.py` at every invocation (exit 6), a TTL where the provider
  has one, a watchdog where it does not.
* *An instance that exists is recorded before anything else is done with it* (the launcher writes the record straight after the provider call).

The commands are the same for every host class; `HOST` is `a100 h100 gh200 b200 c-gcp c-vm`, `N` is 1 or 2, `PLACE` a Lambda region or a
GCP zone. Paths are relative to the repository root on the operator machine. The cloud commands are for a person to approve; each of
`p3_launch.sh`, `p3_teardown.sh` and `p3_price_snapshot.sh` prints what it would do and does nothing without `--yes`.

**Step 0, capacity.** Lambda: `tools/paper3_probes/lambda.sh types` (read-only; the four study types with price and regions that have
capacity). GCP has no capacity query: try the zones in order. A launch can fail for capacity; retry later or in the next place.
Capacity changes by the minute (docs/PAPER3_INFRA.md section 1); do not substitute another instance type (design 3.1).

With `--yes` it calls the provider, writes the launch record `~/p3ledger/<lease>.json` **immediately** (an instance that exists is never
untracked), arms the watchdog for the lease as a wall-clock deadline (Lambda: `tools/paper3_probes/lambda_watchdog.sh`; GCP:

**Step 3, the driver, in the background:**
```bash
tools/paper3_campaign/p3_driver.sh ~/p3ledger/<lease>.json          # run it with Bash run_in_background (or nohup); one notification when it ends
```
What it does, in order. The bring-up, every round and every pack are detached stages on the host (`p3_detach.sh`: own session, every
descriptor redirected, the start call returns at once; a stage is finished exactly when its `.rc` file exists; the driver polls with short,
time-bounded SSH calls and a failed poll is retried, never fatal, so a sleeping Mac or a dropped connection costs polls, not the run):

1. **Bring-up** (generated by `p3_plan.py bringup`, section 5): checkout of CAMPAIGN, the lease written to `/etc/paper3-lease`,
   `setup_h100.sh`, **on a GPU host the CUDA-readiness gate** (`p3_cuda_ready.sh`: a tiny CUDA init and kernel, retried every 30 s for up to 10 minutes; see below),
   `probe_power.py --no-load` (hypervisor verdict, CPU-energy sources, clock, lease: evidence for the launch, not a gate), generate
   and verify the inputs, an SF1 smoke `preflight.py` (verdict only), on C-GCP also the pin check and two
   `paper3_gates.py --capture-exclusivity` captures around the quiesce step. Then a verified copy of its logs and reports.
   **The CUDA gate** exists because of smoke run 2 (2026-11-22): a Lambda H100 that showed its GPU in `nvidia-smi` and answered every CUDA call with error 802
   (`cudaErrorSystemNotReady`, "system not yet initialized"), so the GPU engines and the pulses all failed while the CPU engines were fine. It runs right after setup,
   before any data or engine work, records `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=fabric.state,fabric.status` (when listed), `nvidia-smi -q` (serial numbers, UUIDs and bus ids blanked) and
   `systemctl is-active nvidia-fabricmanager` (when the unit exists) in `rerun/bringup-artifacts/cuda-ready*`, retries on any CUDA init failure, and tries
   `sudo systemctl start nvidia-fabricmanager` once if that unit exists and is not running. If CUDA still fails it prints `[FAIL    ] cuda.ready: host GPU not ready (CUDA 802)` and the
   bring-up exits 82. **The driver then collects the gate's records, tears the host down and confirms it, writes `infrastructure_failure` into the launch record and an
   `infrastructure-failure` line into the ledger, and exits 31.** No round has run, so such a launch is not a lost launch (amendment A1 item 2, the lead's decision of
   2026-11-22) and may simply be launched again, in another region if the first one has none left; the cost is the host's time to that point. The gate's log may be opened to diagnose it
   (`.held-logs`, `p3_open_log.sh`, section 8). CPU hosts have no gate.
2. **C-GCP only:** exit 10 until the reviewed allow-list is committed (section 6; for launch 2 or a relaunch the list is already committed, and the review is the comparison at the end of that
   section); then the host is moved to exactly that commit (`--config-commit`).
3. **Each round K of the launch:** one `run_campaign.py` invocation with `--stop-after-round K` (the last round of the launch runs to the
   end), detached. `run_campaign.py` itself repeats a round that fails its gates once and stops after the second failure.
4. **After each round, while nothing runs on the host:** `p3_host_pack.sh` packs the round's directories (and `campaign.json`,
   `invocations.jsonl`, `status.json`) with a manifest of per-file sha256; the driver copies the archive and its hash, compares the archive
   hash, unpacks, and checks every file against the manifest (up to three tries); a marker in `DEST/.verified/` records it; the QA view of what
   arrived is logged. **Why one invocation and a pause per round:** a copy during a timed round would put ssh, disk reads and network on the
   host during measurement (design 3.2: no other workload), and Paper 1 copied after every round. The price is about 7 minutes per round
   (section 2).
5. **End:** an inventory check (the campaign root on the host and the copy here hold the same files by name and size), teardown with
   confirmation (section 10), the launch record copied beside the data without the address, and `LAUNCH-SHA256SUMS` over everything that arrived.

**Step 5, when the driver ends**, by exit code:

| Exit | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | the launch finished, copied, verified, host terminated and confirmed | check `LAUNCH-SHA256SUMS`, copy the launch directory to the second location, run the post-launch audit (section 10) |
| 10 | C-GCP allow-list review needed | section 6; the host keeps running and bills ($12.70/h) |
| 11 | stopped after bring-up on request | do the manual step (the H100 smoke test), then run the driver again without the flag |
| 20 | **held for a person**; the host keeps running; the log line says why | section 9; the watchdog is the backstop |
| 21 | the provider no longer lists the host | section 9 (host lost) |
| 30 | the campaign stopped by its own rule (a round and its repeat both failed their gates) | everything was copied; the host was terminated unless `--on-stop hold`; section 9 |
| 31 | **the host's GPU is not ready** (the bring-up's CUDA gate failed after its retries, e.g. CUDA error 802): an infrastructure failure before round 1 | the host was torn down and confirmed; the record and the ledger say why; no round ran, so launch again (a fresh `p3_launch.sh`, another region if the first has no capacity) |
| 2 | usage or a refused input | read the message; nothing was started |

**Step 6, relaunching a launch once (amendment A1 item 2).** A launch that stopped by its own rule or was lost leaves its square incomplete (design 3.2). When the lead has decided, in a
dated section 12 amendment, that it is launched once more, the replacement is an instance of its own with the same campaign root and launch number on its new host
(`run_campaign.py --root rerun/paper3-HOST-lN --paper3-launch N`), and everything on this machine keeps the first launch apart from it:

| | first launch | its relaunch |
|---|---|---|
| lease (instance name, `launch` label) | `p3-campaign-cvm-l1-owner-ejiod-expires-...` | `p3-campaign-cvm-l1r2-owner-ejiod-expires-...` |
| launch record | `ordinal` absent (1), `relaunch_of` absent | `ordinal` 2, `relaunch_of` = the old lease |
| local directory | `rerun/paper3/HOST/launchN` | `rerun/paper3/HOST/launchN-r2` (the driver derives it from the record) |
| ledger lines | as before | the same lines with `ordinal` and `relaunch_of`; the launch line has the reason `relaunch 2 of <old lease>` |
| analysis label | `HOST-lN` | `HOST-lNr2` (`paper3_analysis/tree.py` finds the root by its `campaign.json` and numbers the instances of one launch by `created_utc`) |

```bash
tools/paper3_campaign/p3_price_snapshot.sh --out rerun/paper3/snapshots/$(date -u +%Y%m%dT%H%MZ)-HOST-lNr2 --yes
tools/paper3_campaign/p3_launch.sh HOST N --place PLACE --commit CAMPAIGN --price-snapshot rerun/paper3/snapshots/... --credit-funded yes|no \
    --relaunch-of ~/p3ledger/<old lease>.json                 # dry run: prints the request and what it checked; add --yes to launch
tools/paper3_campaign/p3_driver.sh ~/p3ledger/<new lease>.json     # in the background, as for any launch
```
`--relaunch-of` is refused, also in the dry run and before anything is launched, unless the old record has `terminate_confirmed_utc` (the old instance no longer bills) **and**
`rerun/paper3/HOST/launchN/LAUNCH-SHA256SUMS` exists and every file it lists is there with its hash (the old data is copied and intact; `--old-dest DIR` if it is somewhere else),
and it is refused when the ledger already holds a relaunch of that HOST and N: **a launch is relaunched once** (a second one is a new decision and a new tool change). CAMPAIGN is the
commit the replacement is to run: after a defect fixed in the tools it is the commit that holds the fix and its amendment; it must be on `origin` and descend from the lock commit,
as for any launch. A plain `p3_launch.sh` of a launch that already has a record prints a note pointing at `--relaunch-of`, and a driver never writes into a directory that holds another
lease's copy (a `.lease` marker, or a `launch-record.json` of another lease): the first launch's data cannot be overwritten by a second driver. `p3_archive.sh HOST N-r2` and
`p3_ctl.sh pull HOST N-r2` take the relaunch's directory name; `p3_status.sh` says "relaunch 2 of ...". The watchdog, the reaper, the teardown and the ledger work on the record and
the lease name and need nothing else. **C-GCP:** the allow-list review applies to the relaunch as to launch 2: the driver stops with exit 10 until `--config-commit`, and its log carries the
short comparison of section 6 (what the new capture holds that the committed list lacks), so the person has the answer in front of them; the allow-list itself is never changed.

## 5. The host classes

The bring-up commands are generated (`p3_plan.py bringup HOST N --record RECORD` prints the whole script); the lines that differ by class
are below. Bring-up should take about 30 to 45 minutes on a Lambda host (an estimate: 20 to 30 in Paper 1, plus the preflight) and the design budgets 1.8 h per
launch for it (3 h of set-up per host x 1.2 / 2 launches), 1.5 h on C-GCP. Every host runs 26 engine threads (`--cpus 26`), 96 GiB of Spark
driver heap, `a100-parity`, Polars GPU streaming, three measured runs per query, `--query-cleanup none`, 10 s energy windows, as Paper 1 and the
design; `run_campaign.py` fixes them and `--dry-run` prints them. Sessions per round: 32 on a GPU host, 16 on C-GCP, 6 on C-VM.

The run_campaign.py command of a launch is generated from `paper3_campaign.py` and the launch record; below it is shown with placeholders
(`<LEASE_NAME>` and so on come from the record). The stage script appends `--stop-after-round K` for every round but the last of the launch.
`--lease-min-minutes` is one round plus half an hour, so an invocation that cannot finish its round before the lease ends is refused (exit 6)
instead of starting. `--engine-timeout-seconds 7200` is Paper 1's value on every host (the design says only "no timeout"). `--inputs-report` hashes
the two input-gate reports into `campaign.json`. On a host without a GPU `--jar` names a file that is not used.

## 6. C-GCP: the G12 allow-list

G12 on C-GCP is "nothing else scheduled on the host" (design 4). `paper3_gates.py` implements it as: the systemd timers, `/etc/cron.*` files,
the user's crontab entries and `at` jobs seen at the start and end of every round must all be in `paper3_hosts.json`
`hosts.c-gcp.exclusive_use.allowed_scheduled`, a list of item names. At the lock it was `null` (pending), and `run_campaign.py` refuses a formal
C-GCP campaign while it is (`host config unset: ['allowed_scheduled']`). **It was filled once, on 2026-11-23, by amendment A6 (commit 9a8b07b), from what the
bring-up of C-GCP launch 1 captured, before any C-GCP session: 25 items (18 systemd timers and 7 files under `/etc/cron.*`; the capture lists unit and file names whatever
their state, so masked timers are on it too).** It is now fixed for both launches: the host entry's sha256 is part of the campaign's design hash, a resume compares it, and
the second launch's `campaign.json` must carry the same one. **Do not edit it.** The procedure below (steps 1 to 5) is what was followed for launch 1 and is what a **fresh host with no
list yet** would need; for **launch 2 or a relaunch** the list exists, and the review is the short one at the end of this section.

1. **Bring-up produces the evidence.** After setup, inputs and preflight the generated script captures what is scheduled
   (`~/p3out/exclusivity-before.json`), masks the timers in the list below and `cron.service` (a unit that does not exist on the image is
   skipped), and captures again (`exclusivity-after.json`). Both are copied into the launch directory under `rerun/bringup-artifacts/`.
   The masked units are hygiene for a measurement host (apt-daily, apt-daily-upgrade, dpkg-db-backup, e2scrub_all, fstrim, logrotate, man-db,
   motd-news, update-notifier-download, update-notifier-motd, fwupd-refresh, ua-timer, sysstat-collect, sysstat-summary, and, added after launch 1's
   review (amendment A6), apport-autoreport, snapd.snap-repair, systemd-tmpfiles-clean, gce-workload-cert-refresh and google-oslogin-cache: 19 timers, and `cron.service`
   so the files under `/etc/cron.*` are inert); the first fourteen were a guess at Ubuntu 24.04 until the first capture. **Both launches must have the same
   host configuration:** a unit masked by hand during the review is added to `C_GCP_MASK_UNITS` in `p3_plan.py` (committed with the allow-list, which
   the guard permits) so that launch 2's bring-up masks it before its first capture.
2. **The driver stops (exit 10)** with the host running. A person reads `exclusivity-after.json`: every remaining item is either
   something to mask too (preferred: the host should have nothing scheduled) or something to allow with a reason (for example a cron file
   that is inert because `cron.service` is masked). If masking more, repeat the quiesce by hand on the host and capture again
   (`python paper3_gates.py --capture-exclusivity`).
3. **Fill the value** in `paper3_hosts.json` (`allowed_scheduled`: the list of `allow_list_items` that remain, possibly `[]`), nothing else.
   Append the **section 12 amendment** to the design document in the same commit (append-only; sections 1 to 11 must stay byte for byte,
   which `run_campaign.py` verifies on every invocation). Draft:

   > (Template. The amendment that did this for launch 1 is A6, 2026-11-23.)

   > **Reason.** Section 4 G12 requires that nothing else is scheduled on C-GCP. `paper3_hosts.json` `c-gcp.exclusive_use.allowed_scheduled`
   > was `null` (pending) at the lock, so the gate could not be evaluated on that host.
   > **Change.** `allowed_scheduled` = `[...]` (VALUE), from the capture `rerun/bringup-artifacts/exclusivity-after.json` of the first C-GCP
   > boot (sha256 HASH) after the bring-up masked the timers and `cron.service` it lists. The same value applies to both C-GCP launches.
   > No other value of the design or of `paper3_hosts.json` changes.
   > **Outcome data seen.** None: the capture lists scheduled units; no timed C-GCP session had run.
4. **Guard, then continue.** Commit and push (`CONFIG`), then:
   ```bash
   python3 tools/paper3_campaign/p3_plan.py check-allowlist --old CAMPAIGN --new CONFIG     # only c-gcp allowed_scheduled, null -> a list of strings; no other host entry; no harness or gate code differs

   The driver re-runs the same guard, checks the host out at exactly `CONFIG` (fetch, detached checkout, clean tree) and goes on. Every
   other host keeps `CAMPAIGN`; its design digest is its own entry's, so filling in C-GCP changes nothing for them. **A value already set
   is never changed**: the guard refuses a different list, because a value changed mid-campaign would make the two launches different
   designs. If launch 2's boot shows an item that is not in the list, G12 fails that round (an integrity failure: the round is repeated once,
   then the campaign stops); the fix is to mask the item on the host before round 1, never to edit the list.
5. **Host idle time:** the metal host bills while it waits for the review. The review is the one human step in the campaign; do it promptly
   (about $3 to $6 for 15 to 30 minutes).

**Launch 2 of C-GCP, or a relaunch (the list is already in the campaign commit).** The bring-up masks the same 19 timers and `cron.service` as launch 1 did (`C_GCP_MASK_UNITS`), captures,
and the driver still stops with exit 10: the host must not start a formal round on a capture nobody has looked at. The review is whether the new capture holds anything the list does not:
```bash

Nothing printed: continue with `p3_driver.sh ~/p3ledger/<lease>.json --config-commit <the launch's own campaign commit>` (the guard accepts an unchanged filled list, `filled_now: false`; the host is
checked out at that same commit, which is a no-op). An item printed: mask it on the host before round 1 (and add its unit to `C_GCP_MASK_UNITS` for the launches after), **never add it to the list** (the
guard refuses a changed value; G12 would fail the round). The rest of the section does not apply.

**Why not a copy from the GCS archive.** `gs://siriusmedai-gpu-benchmark-archive` (Nearline, public access prevention enforced) holds only SF1
(309 MiB) under `data/`; SF10 to SF300 would have to be generated once and uploaded (about 158 GB) before any copy, and every Lambda launch would then

(docs/PAPER3_INFRA.md section 4) holds for C-GCP: about $3 of host time per launch, and an in-region copy would save little against Hyperdisk's
write limit.

  reads come from the page cache or the disk, both inside that untimed preheat. Do not run `drop_caches`, copy data or read the files
  between sessions.
* **Synthetic readiness data never feeds a campaign.** The harness smoke test (`tools/paper3_smoke/`) writes invented Parquet whose

  its data in its own directory, never under the campaign's `$DATA_ROOT`.
* **The preflight at bring-up** runs the four (or two) engines at SF1 and prints a verdict. Its output directory is
  `rerun/preflight-bringup/` (outside the campaign root); read the verdict line only, and keep it out of any analysis (section 8).

## 8. Blinding (design 7.3) and the QA views

After the lock nobody looks at **engine-versus-engine or host-versus-host energy, power or cost aggregates** until all rounds are completed or
stopped by the rules; stopping, repeating or modifying a round is never based on observed speed, energy or cost.

**Allowed QA views** (design 4): gate outcomes, sample counts, idle stability, coverage, read latencies, overhangs; plus progress (session
counts, the pooled ETA over all engines together, which round is current). The tools show exactly this:

* `tools/paper3_campaign/p3_qa_view.py ROOT [--detail]` reads `status.json`, `round-*/gates.json`, `round-*/paper3-gates.json` and
  `round-*/host-exclusivity-*.json` and **refuses every other path in code** (`ForbiddenRead`; a test tries `results.json`, `gpu.csv`,
  `power.csv`, `host.csv`, `schedule.json`, path tricks). It prints G1 to G5 verdicts, G6 to G12 counts (pass, fail, not evaluable), which gate
  failed on which session key, and the number of scheduled items seen.
* `p3_status.sh` runs the same view on the host plus stage states. The driver logs the view after each round and prints no other values.
* The driver never echoes a stage's log line (`P3_QUIET_LOG=1`, forced): its progress line says only that the stage runs. Of the bring-up log it shows step
  results (`STEP setup ok`, no timestamps), the `[FAIL]` lines of the preflight with every time redacted, and the preflight's `VERDICT` line
  (`tools/paper3_campaign/p3_bringup_view.py`, an allow-list). The full bring-up and round logs are copied off the host and archived unread.
* **The one exception: the log of a stage that failed.** When a stage fails and the driver holds (exit 20) it names the log (`bringup-aN.log`, `campaign-rK-aN.log`)
  and lists it in `.held-logs` in the launch directory. Reading that log to diagnose the failure is permitted, and only through
  `tools/paper3_campaign/p3_open_log.sh RECORD LOGNAME [--tail N]` (from the controller's Mac: `p3_ctl.sh open-log LEASE LOGNAME [N]`), which opens exactly the listed logs,
  refuses every other file (a log that succeeded stays closed), and writes each opening to `log-openings.log` in the launch directory and to the ledger (`held-log-opened`).
  What was seen is "outcome data seen" for the next section 12 amendment (design 7.4). A log opened by hand (`cat`) leaves no record: do not.

**Not allowed, and not opened by anything here:** `results.json` and everything under `round-*/<suite>/` (per-query times, energy windows,
counter readings, outputs), `gpu.csv`, `power.csv`, `host.csv`, `schedule.json` (session durations), the `run` block of `gates.json`
(suite start and finish times), the preflight's own report beyond its verdict, `status.json`'s per-session durations, and any `analyze_*.py`
output on Paper 3 data. Pooled copies are fine to move; do not open them. Do not compute a host's time ratio, a price ratio, or a cost
per query. Do not compare how fast two hosts or two engines progress (the pooled ETA is a sum over all engines for exactly that reason).
The bring-up SF1 smoke and the C-VM time check produce timings too; they are in the same boat until the end.

If a value is seen by accident, write what was seen and when into the next section 12 amendment (design 7.4: "outcome data seen");
an amendment made after an aggregate was seen cannot make the changed element confirmatory.

## 10. Teardown and confirmation

* **The driver does it** at the end (and after a stop by rule): Lambda `lambda.sh terminate ID` (refuses any instance not named `p3-*`), GCP
  `gcloud compute instances delete NAME --zone ZONE --quiet` only while the instance is listed with our label; then it polls the provider
  until the instance is no longer listed, or is listed with status `terminated` (up to twenty minutes, `P3_TERMINATE_WAIT`: Lambda shows a terminated host as `terminating` for a long while; a failed listing is never read as "gone"), writes `terminate_requested_utc` and
  `terminate_confirmed_utc` into the record and two lines into the ledger, and stops the watchdog. A termination that cannot be confirmed holds
  the driver (exit 20) with the watchdog still armed.
* **By hand:** `tools/paper3_campaign/p3_teardown.sh ~/p3ledger/<lease>.json --yes` does the same for a launch whose driver died or that
  never had one (it prints what it would do without `--yes`). After a manual teardown, copy the record beside the data again:
  `jq 'del(.ip, .watchdog_pid)' RECORD > DEST/launch-record.json`.
* **Confirm independently** after every launch, with the read-only commands of section 4, that the provider shows nothing of ours:
  Lambda `lambda.sh list` empty for our names; GCP `gcloud compute instances list --filter='labels.project=paper3-energy'` empty and
  `gcloud compute disks list --filter='name~^p3-campaign- AND -users:*'` empty (the boot disk is created with the instance and deleted with it; a host the provider
  stopped keeps its disk, attached to the stopped instance, until `p3_teardown.sh` deletes the instance, which deletes the disk too).
* **Evidence per launch** (under `rerun/paper3/<host>/launch<N>/`): the campaign root (`rerun/paper3-<host>-l<N>/`), the bring-up reports and
  logs, every stage's log (`bringup-aN.log`, `campaign-rK-aN.log`), `driver-*.log`, `launch-record.json` (with the terminate time), the stage
  scripts (`.stage-scripts/`), `LAUNCH-SHA256SUMS`. The ledger itself stays in `~/p3ledger/`.

3. **The G12 allow-list was not in the locked design or in the locked `paper3_hosts.json`** (a `null`). It was filled as a pending value by amendment A6 on 2026-11-23 (commit 9a8b07b),

4. **`campaign.json` cannot hold the terminate time** that design 2.6 lists (it is written once, at the start). The operator's record
   (`launch-record.json`, copied beside the data) holds it; the analysis must read it from there.
5. **Price snapshots (design 2.6)**: "at the lock and at the start and end of each host's campaign". The repository's evidence holds only the

   Cloud Billing API enabled (consent, docs/PAPER3_INFRA.md section 5). The hourly audit and the ledger are the controls.
7. **Hyperdisk 16,000 IOPS and 1,000 MBps on C3 metal and VM are unverified**, as is `--cpu-only` with `--enable-rapl-read` as one call on a real
   host. The first C-GCP launch is their real test; both fail loudly.
8. **The preflight smoke at bring-up runs study queries at SF1 on the campaign host** (Paper 1 practice). It is not a pilot if nobody looks
   at it (section 8), but the lead should confirm that is acceptable under design 7.2's "no pilot of study queries on instrumented hosts"; it
   writes outside the campaign root and a verdict is all that is read.
9. **`campaign.json` did not record the hypervisor and the storage type** that design 3.1 lists. Found while writing this runbook and fixed in
   `run_campaign.py`: in Paper 3 mode `host` now also holds `hypervisor` (`systemd-detect-virt`) and `storage` (the device under the data root from
   `findmnt` and `lsblk`: parent disk, rotational, type, transport, model; no serial numbers); Paper 1 campaigns are unchanged
   (`tests/test_paper3_host_identity.py`). The provisioned Hyperdisk IOPS and throughput are not visible from inside the host; the launch
   record holds the request (`boot_disk_requested`) and what the provider reports back after the create (`boot_disk_actual`).
10. **Pausing between rounds** is this runbook's choice (design 3.2 says Paper 1 controls: off-host rsync and checksums after every round, without
   saying when). The alternative, copying during the next round, would load the host during measurement. The cost is about $30 over the campaign
   and 0.5 to 1 hour per launch.

The author (or the lead) must decide before the first launch:

* `--cost-credit-funded` for GCP (`yes` or `no`): the funding statement names Lambda and AWS credits only. Lambda defaults to `yes`.
* **Relaunching a square.** A launch that stops by rule or is lost leaves its square incomplete (design 3.2: confirmatory estimates use complete
  squares only; "no round is added or dropped for what its values look like"). The tools relaunch a launch once, on the lead's decision (section 4, step 6); the decision is a dated
  section 12 amendment first (A1 item 2: a replacement after a technical failure, the stopped rounds reported and never pooled). Nothing relaunches by itself.
* **When capacity never allows a second launch.** `run_campaign.py --paper3-launch both` runs all eight rounds on one instance (design 3.2: separate
  launches "where capacity allows"). The tools here handle launches 1 and 2 only; supporting `both` means a third root name and a longer lease in the
  planner. Not done; the lead decides when a second launch has been impossible for too long.
* Whether to run the Lambda drivers from an always-on machine instead of the Mac (the watchdog and the polling live on the operator machine;
  a 27-hour A100 launch on a laptop is a risk that GCP's provider-side limit does not share): `docs/PAPER3_CONTROLLER.md` recommends it, with the GCP launches
  staying on the Mac under `P3_GCP_TERMINATION_ACTION=STOP`; the author decides.
* Whether the harness smoke test and the lock-date price snapshot are done before the pathfinders start (they are prerequisites in this runbook).
